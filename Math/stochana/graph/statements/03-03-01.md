## statement

A stochastic process M = (Mt ) on a filtered probability space (Ω, F, (Ft ), P) is called a local martingale if there exists a localising sequence of stopping times {τn }, that is, a sequence of stopping times {τn } such that • τn ↑ ∞ a.s. • the stopped process M τn = Mτn ∧⋅ is a martingale w.r.t. (Ft ) for every n ∈ N. The space of all continuous local martingales is denoted Mloc ; the space of continuous local martingales starting in zero is denoted M0loc . Localising sequences are not unique. For instance, if {τn } is a localising sequence, then so is {τn ∧ n}.
